Soft60 · daily regulation

A nervous system routine that does not become another job.

The point is not to perform calm beautifully. The point is to make it easy enough to repeat.

If the phrase “nervous system regulation routine” makes you picture a two-hour morning protocol, I would like to offer the opposite.

A useful routine should make your life easier to inhabit. It should not require a spreadsheet, twelve supplements, and a personality transplant.

The Soft60 version is intentionally small

The method uses six daily practices: regulate, subtract chaos, move gently, eat with presence, create identity proof, and reflect.

You do not have to do them in a perfect order. You do not have to make them photogenic. You just need enough repetition for your days to stop feeling completely improvised.

Morning: regulate before input

Before you start consuming everyone else’s thoughts, give yourself a few minutes without input. Breathe. Sit quietly. Stretch. Walk. Look out the window. Pick one.

This is not about achieving a special state. It is about beginning the day without immediately handing your attention away.

Daytime: subtract and move

Remove one piece of unnecessary chaos. Then move your body in a way that feels supportive rather than punitive.

A short walk counts. Stretching counts. Strength training counts. The important part is the relationship you are practicing with yourself while you do it.

At one meal: be there

Put the phone away and let one meal be an actual pause. Sit down. Taste the food. Stop making every quiet moment into another chance to consume information.

Evening: collect evidence

Ask two questions: what did I do today that made my life calmer, and what did I do that proved the person I am becoming?

That is enough for a reflection. One line is enough.

A routine is useful when it lowers friction, not when it gives you another way to grade yourself.

What if you miss a day?

Do not turn one missed practice into a three-week identity crisis. Return the next day. The skill is not perfection. The skill is returning without drama.
