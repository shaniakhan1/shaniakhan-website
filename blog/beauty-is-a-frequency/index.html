Beauty Is a Frequency: Before the Mirror Changes

By Shania Khan

← Back to Journal

I do not believe beauty begins with fixing yourself. I think that is where a lot of beauty advice gets it wrong. It starts with the assumption that there is a problem, then hands you a shopping list.

Beauty Is a Frequency came from noticing that the women I find most beautiful are not always the most conventionally perfect. They are the women who are fully inside themselves. Their clothes look intentional. Their hair looks cared for. They move like they have time. There is congruence between how they feel, how they live, and what they present to the world.

Beauty is a relationship, not a punishment

If your beauty routine is built on disgust, it will always feel like maintenance on a problem. If it is built on self-respect, the same actions feel completely different. Washing your face. Putting on the dress you love on a Tuesday. Drinking water out of the glass you save for company. Buying fewer things and choosing better ones.

That is frequency to me. It is the state underneath the ritual.

The mirror follows the life

I have had seasons where I bought more products and felt worse. I have had seasons where nothing dramatic changed physically, but I looked better because I was sleeping, eating properly, dressing with intention, saying no faster, and no longer carrying ten unnecessary emergencies in my body.

Beauty is affected by the life around it. Your posture changes when you are not bracing. Your face changes when you are not exhausted. Your taste gets clearer when you stop buying from panic. You become easier to recognize because you are no longer trying on ten identities at once.

Standards are part of beauty

Beauty is also what you stop tolerating. The appointment you keep. The fabric you refuse to wear because it feels terrible on your body. The room you clean because you deserve to wake up somewhere that feels good. The relationship you stop allowing to drain the life out of your face.

This is why I say Beauty Is a Frequency. It is not a number on a scale or a before-and-after photo. It is a practiced relationship with yourself. The mirror eventually reflects it, but the mirror is not where it begins.
